## Summary
This workshop will focus on the neural mechanisms underlying altered states of consciousness,
exploring findings from both classical EEG methods and advanced brain-computer interfaces
(BCIs). By addressing conditions such as general anesthesia, locked-in syndrome, disorders of
consciousness, and states induced by hypnosis or pharmacological agents, the complementary
talks aim to highlight the neuronal processes driving these states and their implications for both
basic neuroscience and clinical practice.
The workshop will feature five expert speakers who will present recent advances in EEG-based
techniques for monitoring awareness during anesthesia, detecting neural signatures of
response attempts in locked-in patients, patients with disorders of consciousness or other
altered states. The workshop will also address technical challenges such as reliable signal
acquisition, data interpretation in different clinical and experimental settings, and the integration
of classical and innovative methods.
Designed for researchers, clinicians and engineers in neuroscience, neurotechnology and
related disciplines, this half-day workshop provides an interdisciplinary platform to discuss the
opportunities and challenges of using EEG and BCI technologies. Participants will gain a deeper
understanding of consciousness research and its potential to improve both scientific knowledge
and patients’ outcomes.

## Rationale
The study of altered states of consciousness using EEG, including both classical methods and
brain-computer interfaces (BCIs), is a rapidly developing field with profound implications for
neuroscience and clinical practice. By addressing conditions such as general anesthesia,
locked-in syndrome, coma, and states induced by hypnosis or pharmacological agents, this
workshop provides a unique platform to investigate the cortical mechanisms underlying sensory
processing and cognitive functions across varieties of conscious and unconscious states.
Classical EEG approaches and advanced neurotechnological tools, including BCIs, offer
complementary perspectives, enabling the development of innovative methods for monitoring,
diagnosing and prognosticating brain states.
This topic is particularly relevant to the EMBS community as it bridges basic neuroscience
research with critical healthcare applications. Both traditional EEG analysis and cutting-edge
BCI technologies have transformative potential to improve patient care, for example by
improving the monitoring of depth of anaesthesia, facilitating communication in locked-in
patients, or aiding in the assessment of recovery in coma patients. The interdisciplinary nature
of this research is in line with the EMBS mission to advance medicine and biology through
technology, fostering collaboration between engineers, clinicians and neuroscientists to address
both technical and ethical challenges.
By combining classical and novel approaches, this workshop aims to promote progress in the
understanding and management of consciousness, opening up new avenues for scientific
discovery and clinical innovation.
 
## Duration
The workshop will take place over a half day with five expert speakers. This compact format will
enable focused presentations and discussions on the latest advances in EEG observations and
brain-computer interfaces in understanding altered states of consciousness and their
applications. Attendees will have the opportunity to engage with the speakers, participate in
in-depth discussions and gain a thorough understanding of the topic.
